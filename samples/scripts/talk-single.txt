# talk-single: one speaker, about 2 minutes. Fictional product "Lumenstream" (invented for this sample).
# Format: "Voice: text" per line. Lines starting with # and blank lines are ignored.
Matthew: Good morning, everyone. Thank you for joining this session. My name is Alex, and today I will walk you through Lumenstream, a fictional video delivery platform that we built for live events.
Matthew: Let me start with the problem. When you stream a live concert or a sports match to a million viewers, the hardest part is not the video quality. It is keeping the latency low while the audience grows from a few thousand to hundreds of thousands of people in just a few minutes.
Matthew: Lumenstream solves this with three building blocks. The first is the ingest layer. Encoders send the stream to us over SRT, and we accept it at the edge closest to the venue. The second is the packaging layer. We transcode the stream into a bitrate ladder, and we package it as HLS and DASH, with segments as short as one second.
Matthew: The third building block is the delivery layer. We use a multi-CDN strategy, so if one provider has a problem, the player switches to another one automatically. The player SDK measures throughput on every segment, and it reports the p99 latency back to our observability dashboard.
Matthew: Now, a few words about how we run this in production. Every component is a container that runs on Kubernetes. We use autoscaling based on the number of concurrent viewers, and we keep a warm pool of transcoders, so a surge does not wait for new machines to start.
Matthew: We also learned some lessons the hard way. First, always test failover before the event, not during it. Second, keep your manifests small, because a large manifest slows down every player on a weak mobile network. Third, give your operators a single dashboard, with one page that tells them whether the stream is healthy.
Matthew: Finally, a note on cost. Because the transcoders scale down when the event is over, a typical two-hour event costs less than a single day of a fixed cluster. Customers told us that this was the main reason they switched.
Matthew: To summarize, low latency comes from short segments, a good ingest path, and a player that adapts quickly. Thank you very much for your attention. I am happy to take your questions after the session.
